# GlucareOn -- Brand Plan

*This is a fictional, illustrative brand-plan document, written to exercise the
kit-update ingestion flow. Nothing in it describes a real product, a real clinical
trial, or real patient/physician data. It is not approved content and must never be
treated as one.*

## Who GlucareOn is

GlucareOn talks like an endocrinologist who has run the numbers on cost, not just
efficacy, because they know a patient who can't afford to fill the prescription gets
zero benefit from a great A1C reduction on a slide. It's practical, a little
no-nonsense, and allergic to anything that sounds like it's minimizing how hard
day-to-day diabetes management actually is for the person living it. If GlucareOn were
a person, they'd be the endocrinologist who asks about a patient's actual grocery
budget before asking about their diet.

## The clinical story, in plain terms

GlucareOn is positioned for adults with type 2 diabetes inadequately controlled on
metformin alone, with a secondary emphasis on cardiovascular risk reduction in patients
who also have established cardiovascular disease. The pitch is meaningful A1C
lowering plus a cardiovascular benefit that most competing oral options in its class
can't claim, delivered in a once-weekly format that removes daily pill-taking from the
list of things a patient has to remember.

## HCP personas

### Dr. Linda Osei -- "The Whole-Patient Endocrinologist"

Linda runs a diabetes-focused endocrinology practice and has become known among her
referring primary care colleagues for spending real time on the social determinants
that actually predict whether a regimen works -- cost, complexity, and whether a
patient can realistically fit it into their life. Her biggest daily frustration is
prescribing something clinically appropriate only to learn three months later that the
patient quietly stopped filling it because of copay sticker shock at the pharmacy
counter, something she often doesn't find out about until the next A1C comes back
unchanged. She wants a brand rep to lead with formulary tier and copay assistance
program details before efficacy data -- not because she doubts the science, but because
she's learned the hard way that the best drug on paper is worthless if it never
actually reaches the patient. She reads real-world evidence studies as closely as
randomized trial data, specifically because she cares how a therapy performs outside
the controlled conditions of a trial, in patients who look like the ones actually
sitting in her exam room.

### Dr. Tomas Alvarez -- "The Cardiometabolic Connector"

Tomas is a primary care physician managing a large type 2 diabetes population
alongside a significant number of patients with existing cardiovascular disease, and
he's become increasingly focused on choosing diabetes therapies specifically for their
cardiovascular risk-reduction data, not just glycemic control, because he sees his role
as managing the whole cardiometabolic picture rather than treating A1C as an isolated
number. He's genuinely skeptical of any new therapy whose data package doesn't include
a dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial -- he considers that data non-negotiable for
a patient population he already thinks of as high cardiovascular risk by default. What
would move his prescribing fastest is a therapy with clean, published cardiovascular
outcomes data in a population that actually resembles his patients, plus a dosing
schedule simple enough that he's not asking an already-overwhelmed patient to add
another daily task to a regimen that may already include multiple other medications.

## Brand voice notes

GlucareOn's tone pillars: practical, cost-aware, respectful of daily burden, never
paternalistic. The brand deliberately never uses "simply" or "just" when describing
what a patient needs to do -- lived diabetes management is not simple, and language that
implies otherwise reads as dismissive to both physicians and patients. Every efficacy
claim in GlucareOn material is paired with real-world access context (formulary status,
patient support program availability) rather than presented as a standalone number.
Internally the brand calls this "the number and the path to the number."
