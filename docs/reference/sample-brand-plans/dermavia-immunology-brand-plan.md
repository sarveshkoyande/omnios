# DermaVia -- Brand Plan

*This is a fictional, illustrative brand-plan document, written to exercise the
kit-update ingestion flow. Nothing in it describes a real product, a real clinical
trial, or real patient/physician data. It is not approved content and must never be
treated as one.*

## Who DermaVia is

DermaVia sounds like the dermatologist who actually asks patients how a flare affects
their life before talking about a treatment plan -- not performatively, but because the
answer genuinely changes what they'd recommend. It's warm without being soft on
evidence, and it treats "itch score" and "quality of life" as equally real endpoints,
not one clinical and one soft. If DermaVia were a person, colleagues would describe
them as the dermatologist who remembers which of their patients has a wedding coming
up.

## The clinical story, in plain terms

DermaVia is positioned for adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis who have
had an inadequate response to topical therapy. The pitch centers on rapid itch relief
-- because itch, more than lesion size, is what patients say actually ruins their
day-to-day -- paired with a safety monitoring profile simple enough for a community
dermatologist to manage without a specialist referral.

## HCP personas

### Dr. Amara Boateng -- "The Itch-First Clinician"

Amara runs a busy general dermatology practice and has become something of an
evangelist, among her peers, for treating itch as a primary outcome rather than a
symptom that resolves once lesions clear. She's watched patients describe genuinely
life-altering sleep disruption from itch that a lesion-severity score alone completely
fails to capture, and it's changed how she evaluates every new therapy that crosses her
desk. Her main hesitation with newer systemic options isn't efficacy -- it's monitoring
burden. She's reluctant to start a therapy that requires lab work she can't easily order
and track in her own practice, because she knows in practice that means either a
specialist referral her patients often skip, or monitoring that quietly falls through
the cracks. She wants a brand to lead with two things: how fast itch actually improves,
in patient-reported terms, and exactly what monitoring is required and whether she can
run it herself. She prefers case-based conference sessions over straight data
presentations -- she says she remembers a patient story longer than she remembers a
forest plot.

### Dr. Ravi Chandrasekaran -- "The Pediatric Advocate"

Ravi splits his practice between adult and pediatric dermatology and has a particular
focus on adolescent atopic dermatitis patients, where he says the disease's social and
psychological toll is often worse than the visible skin involvement -- teenagers miss
school, avoid swimming, and describe real social anxiety tied to visible flares. He is
notably cautious about new systemic therapies in younger patients specifically, and
will not move a patient onto one without long-term safety data he's personally
comfortable defending to a worried parent in the room. He reads pediatric-specific
subanalyses closely when they exist and is openly frustrated when a brand's data
package treats "adults and adolescents" as one undifferentiated population without
breaking out the numbers. What would change his prescribing fastest is a therapy with a
genuinely simple, teen-compatible dosing schedule and an honest, specific safety
conversation he can have with a 15-year-old and their parent in the same visit without
talking past either of them.

## Brand voice notes

DermaVia's tone pillars: warm, patient-centered, rigorously specific, never
minimizing. The brand deliberately avoids treating "clear skin" as the only outcome
that matters -- itch relief and sleep quality get equal billing in every core message.
DermaVia never uses "safe" as a bare adjective; every safety claim names the specific
monitoring or population it applies to. Internally this is called "no safety claim
without its asterisk."
