# OncoLine -- Brand Plan

*This is a fictional, illustrative brand-plan document, written to exercise the
kit-update ingestion flow. Nothing in it describes a real product, a real clinical
trial, or real patient/physician data. It is not approved content and must never be
treated as one.*

## Who OncoLine is

OncoLine reads like it was written by someone who has actually sat in a tumor board
and watched a treatment decision get made in real time, under real pressure, with a
real patient's chart open on the screen. It doesn't perform empathy -- it demonstrates
competence, and trusts that competence to read as care. It's blunt about uncertainty
where uncertainty exists, and precise about data where the data is precise. If OncoLine
were a colleague, it would be the oncologist other oncologists quietly ask for a second
opinion.

## The clinical story, in plain terms

OncoLine is positioned for a molecularly-defined subset of advanced solid tumor
patients who have progressed on prior lines of therapy and have a specific biomarker
alteration confirmed by testing. The core pitch is durable response in a population
that has historically had few options once first-line therapy fails, paired with a
tolerability profile that lets patients keep working through treatment where that
matters to them.

## HCP personas

### Dr. Sofia Reyes -- "The Molecular Detective"

Sofia is a medical oncologist at an academic cancer center who has built her entire
practice philosophy around one idea: test everything, guess at nothing. She pushes
hard -- sometimes harder than her tumor board colleagues are comfortable with -- to get
every eligible patient molecularly profiled at diagnosis, because she's watched too
many patients miss a targetable alteration simply because nobody ordered the right
panel in time. Her single biggest frustration with the whole field isn't any one drug,
it's testing turnaround time: by the time a biomarker result comes back, a patient has
sometimes already started a line of therapy that closes off a better option. She wants
to hear from a brand exactly one thing before anything else: what's the confirmed
biomarker prevalence in the population, and how fast can testing realistically turn
around. She trusts peer-reviewed data and conference abstracts far more than a sales
deck, and she's explicit that she'd rather read the actual Kaplan-Meier curve herself
than have someone narrate it to her. "Show me the curve," she says. "I'll draw my own
conclusions."

### Dr. James Okafor -- "The Community Bridge"

James practices community oncology, seeing a broad mix of tumor types without the
luxury of a dedicated molecular pathology team down the hall. He relies heavily on
outside labs for biomarker testing and has learned to plan his treatment sequencing
around lab turnaround delays that his academic colleagues sometimes forget exist
outside their own institutions. His patients are often older, managing multiple
comorbidities, and traveling a real distance to get to his clinic, so he weighs
tolerability and dosing convenience more heavily than a strictly efficacy-only
calculation would suggest -- a patient who can't tolerate a regimen long enough to see
benefit gets zero benefit from it. He values a brand rep who can talk him through
practical logistics: what specimen type the biomarker test needs, which reference labs
run it, and what a typical turnaround actually looks like in the real world, not the
best case. "I don't need the drug that wins in a vacuum," he says. "I need the one my
patients can actually stay on."

## Brand voice notes

OncoLine's tone pillars: precise, unflinching about uncertainty, quietly rigorous,
never triumphant. The brand explicitly avoids "cure," "miracle," or any language
implying an outcome beyond what the data supports -- oncology audiences read overreach
as either naivety or dishonesty, and OncoLine can't afford to be read as either. Every
efficacy statement is paired with the specific patient population it applies to,
never presented as a general claim. Internally, the brand calls this discipline
"the population travels with the number."
