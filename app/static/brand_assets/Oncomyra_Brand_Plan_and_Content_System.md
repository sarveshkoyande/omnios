# 1. Executive Summary

**What Oncomyra™ is (source-supported):** Oncomyra™ (talrenimab) is a first-in-class* anti-FGFR2 antibody–drug conjugate (ADC). Rather than blocking the intracellular ATP-binding pocket like FGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), it binds the **extracellular domain** of FGFR2, internalizes, and releases a **microtubule-disrupting cytotoxic payload** directly into FGFR2-expressing tumor cells (REF-01, REF-02). *"First-in-class" is source-stated but **High** MLR risk — see Section 11.

**Proposed / studied indication (source-supported):** Adults with **previously treated** (≥1 prior line of gemcitabine-based therapy), **unresectable locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma** harboring an **FGFR2 fusion or rearrangement** (REF-01, REF-03). *First-line combination use is investigational only — see barriers below.*

**The brand opportunity.** FGFR2 fusions define a distinct, testable molecular subset of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) — ~10–16% of patients (REF-01, REF-05) / 14.8% of screened patients in the biomarker analysis (REF-02) — a poor-prognosis population with limited durable options after first-line chemotherapy. CLARITY-01 is positioned as the **first Phase III study to show an overall survival (OS) benefit for an FGFR2-targeted ADC vs active chemotherapy** in this subset (REF-01). The commercial and medical opportunity is therefore inseparable from **biomarker testing uptake**: every eligible patient must first be *identified*.

**Primary challenge.** Two challenges dominate: (1) **patient identification** — universal, RNA-based NGS testing at diagnosis is not yet routine in all settings (REF-02); and (2) **safety orchestration** — hyperphosphatemia and ocular events are on-target/class effects requiring proactive monitoring, with additional considerations in renal, hepatic, and cardiovascular comorbidity (REF-03, REF-06, REF-07, REF-08). The brand must make testing and monitoring *easy*, not just make efficacy *known*.

**Desired brand role.** The credible, precise partner for the FGFR2-positive iCCA decision: *test early, treat with an OS-supported targeted mechanism, manage proactively.*

**Key strategic direction.**
1. Lead the market on **"test at diagnosis"** for FGFR2 fusions (largely unbranded / disease-education territory).
2. Anchor the branded story in **CLARITY-01 OS**, always with fair balance.
3. Convert the safety profile from a barrier into a **manageability** proof point via monitoring algorithms and special-population guidance.
4. Keep first-line combination data strictly **medical/scientific and investigational** — never promotional.

**Communication priorities:** (a) disease + testing education; (b) CLARITY-01 efficacy with balanced safety; (c) MOA differentiation vs TKIs; (d) special-population confidence (renal/hepatic/CV/elderly); (e) practical monitoring enablement.

**Major guardrails.** No survival/cure overpromising; no unsupported superiority beyond the FOLFOX comparison actually studied; no first-line/combination promotion; no minimization of hyperphosphatemia/ocular risk; strict commercial↔medical separation; **do not publish any dosing until the source dose conflict is resolved.**

**What the brand must do well:** make FGFR2 testing routine; communicate OS credibly and honestly; equip HCPs to monitor and dose-manage.
**What the brand must avoid:** superlatives without substantiation ("best," "breakthrough," "game-changing"); implying benefit outside the studied 2L+ population; head-to-head superiority vs other FGFR inhibitors (none exists); emotional patient manipulation.

# 3. Brand Situation Assessment

### Market context (source-supported)
iCCA is a rare, aggressive biliary malignancy; FGFR2 fusions occur in ~10–16% of iCCA and act as oncogenic drivers (REF-01, REF-05). First-line standard is gemcitabine + cisplatin (± immunotherapy), with historical mOS ~11–12 months (REF-05). Prior FGFR TKIs show activity but are limited by acquired resistance (gatekeeper/secondary kinase mutations) and off-target toxicity, and have struggled to show OS benefit over chemotherapy in randomized settings (REF-01).

### Disease burden & unmet need (source-supported)
Poor prognosis after first-line therapy; resistance and toxicity limit TKI durability; OS benefit historically hard to demonstrate (REF-01). Comorbidity is common — CKD from prior platinum/obstruction (REF-06), hepatic impairment from tumor/biliary obstruction (REF-08), and cardiovascular disease from age/prior therapy (REF-07).

### Patient journey challenges (source-adapted)
- Under-testing / delayed molecular profiling → eligible patients missed (REF-02 recommends universal testing).
- Liquid-biopsy false-negatives in low-shedding disease → risk of denying therapy without reflex tissue testing (REF-02).
- Comorbidity-driven dosing complexity (renal/hepatic) (REF-06, REF-08).
- Monitoring burden: phosphate + ophthalmologic surveillance (REF-03).

### HCP decision-making barriers (source-adapted)
- Whether/when to test, and by which method (NGS vs liquid) (REF-02).
- Confidence managing hyperphosphatemia and ocular toxicity (REF-03).
- Confidence dosing in special populations (REF-06, REF-07, REF-08).
- Interpreting an open-label OS result (design limitation; PFS mitigated by BICR) (REF-01).

### Competitive context (source-limited)
Source names prior FGFR agents contextually — pemigatinib, infigratinib, futibatinib/TAS-120 (reference lists of REF-01, REF-02, REF-05). **No head-to-head comparison vs any FGFR TKI exists in these documents.** → Any competitive positioning must be **mechanistic/contextual only**, never a comparative efficacy/safety claim. *Requires competitive intelligence input.*

### Access / reimbursement (not in source)
No pricing, payer, coverage, or economic data provided. → **Source support not found in provided materials.** Any payer content is a strategic placeholder requiring market-access input.

### Situation synthesis

| Lens | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **What the brand does well** | Randomized Phase III OS + PFS + ORR benefit vs chemo (REF-01); efficacy consistent across FGFR2 fusion partners (REF-02); mechanistic rationale to overcome TKI kinase-domain resistance (REF-01); usable data in renal/hepatic/CV/elderly subgroups (REF-04, REF-06, REF-07, REF-08); defined AE-management algorithms (REF-03). |
| **Where the brand may struggle** | Dependence on testing uptake; monitoring burden (phosphate/ocular); comorbidity dosing complexity; open-label pivotal design; **unresolved dosing + DDI source conflicts**; no access/economic evidence. |
| **What this means strategically** | Win the *testing* battle and the *manageability* battle, not just the efficacy argument. |
| **Implications for communication** | Pair every efficacy message with fair balance and a monitoring/management pathway; heavy investment in unbranded disease/testing education. |
| **Implications for content** | Modular safety + monitoring assets are as important as efficacy assets; special-population modules are a differentiator. |
| **Implications for omnichannel** | Trigger testing-education early in the journey; sequence efficacy → safety → practical-use → support; reserve first-line/combination content for medical channels only. |

# 7. Brand Story

> Short/long/HCP/internal versions are drafts for internal alignment. **Patient version is gated** — see §15.

**The tension (source-supported).** FGFR2 fusion–positive iCCA is a distinct, aggressive subset with poor prognosis after first-line chemotherapy. Targeted TKIs brought progress but are limited by acquired kinase-domain resistance and off-target toxicity, and OS benefit over chemotherapy has been hard to prove (REF-01).

**The clinical need.** A targeted mechanism that can work across the many FGFR2 fusion partners, is less vulnerable to secondary kinase mutations, and delivers a *survival* benefit — while remaining manageable in the comorbid patients seen in practice.

**The role of innovation / Oncomyra™.** Oncomyra™ (talrenimab) approaches FGFR2 differently: as an ADC that binds the receptor's *extracellular* domain and delivers a cytotoxic payload inside the cell — a mechanism hypothesized to be less dependent on the kinase pocket that drives TKI resistance (REF-01, REF-02).

**The evidence foundation.** CLARITY-01 (Phase III) showed a statistically significant OS and PFS benefit vs FOLFOX, with responses consistent across fusion partners (REF-01, REF-02), supported by defined AE-management algorithms (REF-03) and characterization across renal, hepatic, cardiovascular, and real-world populations (REF-04, REF-06, REF-07, REF-08).

**Short version (HCP-facing, ~40 words):**
> In FGFR2 fusion–positive cholangiocarcinoma, Oncomyra™ is an FGFR2-directed ADC that significantly prolonged overall survival vs chemotherapy in CLARITY-01 — with fusion-partner-agnostic response and defined management pathways. *Review full ISI, including hyperphosphatemia and ocular events.* (Requires MLR.)

**Internal-stakeholder version:**
> Our right to win rests on three pillars: a differentiated ADC mechanism, a randomized Phase III OS result, and a manageability story for real-world comorbid patients. Our two hardest jobs are getting patients *tested* and getting HCPs *confident to monitor and dose*. Everything we build should advance one of those. We do not promote first-line/combination data, we do not claim superiority beyond the FOLFOX comparison, and we do not ship dosing until the source conflict is resolved.

**HCP long version:** Combines Sections 3, 5, and the messaging pillars in §10; always closes with fair balance and a testing/monitoring call to action.

# 9. Brand Guardrails

### 9.1 Strategic guardrails
- **Emphasize:** FGFR2 testing at diagnosis; CLARITY-01 OS/PFS/ORR with fair balance; MOA differentiation (mechanistic, not comparative-efficacy); manageability & special-population data.
- **Avoid:** superlatives; first-line/combination promotion; superiority vs other FGFR agents; any dosing/DDI statement until source conflict resolved.
- **Always balance:** every efficacy claim with prominent safety (hyperphosphatemia, ocular events, cytopenias, fluid retention) and full ISI.
- **Requires evidence support:** any incidence figure (choose the correct, PI-aligned source), any subgroup claim, any testing-method recommendation.
- **Requires medical review:** MOA nuance, resistance rationale ("may overcome" is hypothesis-level), investigational combination data.
- **Requires legal/regulatory review:** "first-in-class," "standard of care," any comparative framing, any patient-facing or payer-facing asset.

### 9.2 Claim guardrails (hard rules)
- ❌ No unsupported superiority (only the *studied* vs-FOLFOX comparison is supported, with fair balance).
- ❌ No survival claim beyond the exact CLARITY-01 figures and their CIs; no extrapolation to individual patients.
- ❌ No implied cure or "long-term freedom" language.
- ❌ No broadening beyond previously treated FGFR2-positive iCCA.
- ❌ No unsupported quality-of-life claims (no PRO/QoL data provided).
- ❌ No safety minimization; never present efficacy without balance.
- ❌ No unsupported real-world superiority (RWE is descriptive/retrospective — REF-04).
- ❌ No cross-population extrapolation (e.g., applying 2L+ data to 1L, or normal-organ data to severe impairment).
- ❌ No comparison unless source-supported and MLR-approved.

### 9.3 Audience guardrails

| Audience | Must do | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| **HCP** | On-label, balanced, referenced; testing + monitoring pathways | Off-label/first-line promo; superiority beyond FOLFOX |
| **Patient/Caregiver** | Plain, empathetic, "talk to your care team"; balanced risk | Outcome guarantees; fear appeals; complex promo claims without context; **any asset before PI/ISI/reg input** |
| **Payer/Access** | Only source-supported clinical value; clearly labeled gaps | Economic/cost-effectiveness claims (**none in source**) |
| **Field (commercial)** | Approved messages only; open testing/management dialogue | Discuss investigational data; give medical/dosing advice; use unapproved data |
| **Medical (MSL/MI)** | Balanced scientific exchange; respond to unsolicited questions per SOP | Proactively promote; blur into commercial messaging |
| **Commercial (marketing)** | Maintain claim–reference–fair-balance integrity across channels | Reuse medical-only assets in promo; alter data figures |
